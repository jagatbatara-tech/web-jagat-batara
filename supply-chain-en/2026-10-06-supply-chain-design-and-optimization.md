## From strategy to network design

A supply chain should support how the business competes. A low-cost strategy, for example, may prioritize efficient processes and automation, while a market with uncertain demand may require more flexibility and responsiveness. Network decisions should be assessed by their impact on the entire chain—not only on an individual facility or function.

The design includes where suppliers, plants, warehouses, and other resources are located; their capacity and capabilities; and how goods move between them. More warehouses can bring goods closer to customers and reduce delivery times, but they also add inventory and facility costs. Transport choices are affected by infrastructure, distance, capacity, and border processes.

## Efficiency, responsiveness, and resilience

Efficiency and responsiveness involve trade-offs. Stable, predictable demand can support inventory-based fulfillment and efficient operations. Uncertain demand, broader product variety, or shorter product lifecycles may call for faster information, flexible capacity, agile suppliers, and responsive logistics.

Resilience also matters: safety stock, multiple sources of supply, threat indicators, and the ability to react can help protect operations from disruption. Efficiency is important, but it does not remove the need for appropriate resilience.

## Technology as a business decision

Supply chain technology can improve information sharing, visibility, forecasting, and coordination across partners. Investments should be justified by total benefits and total costs, including implementation, training, data conversion, ongoing support, and other less visible expenses. Both tangible and intangible benefits matter; the latter should be estimated with stakeholder agreement.

## Building supply chain maturity

The study describes five maturity stages, from disconnected processes to an orchestrated supply chain with cross-company collaboration and end-to-end visibility. Organizations cannot skip stages: leadership, standardized processes, capable teams, trust between partners, and suitable technology all contribute to progress. The least-developed capability can constrain the wider network.
